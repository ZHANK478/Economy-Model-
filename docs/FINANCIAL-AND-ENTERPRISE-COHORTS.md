# Economic revision 11: sector cohorts, funds and medical insurance

The model is an aggregate fictional economy. Coefficients below are explicit scenario choices, not empirical estimates. Evidence informs mechanisms, not universal effect sizes: OECD [firm heterogeneity and reallocation](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/identifying-the-main-drivers-of-productivity-growth_00435b80-en/full-report/component-6.html), OECD [pension asset allocation](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pensions-at-a-glance-2025_e40274c1-en/full-report/allocation-of-assets_2cfd1e66.html), WHO [pooling medical funds](https://www.who.int/activities/pooling/pooling), WHO [financial protection](https://www.who.int/health-topics/financial-protection).

Each of the five sector producers contains three stylized capital cohorts (young, established and large) with relative productivity assumptions 0.86, 1.00 and 1.08. Their capital shares sum to one and shift only gradually in response to current investment, profitability, competition and financing costs. The resulting change in their weighted productivity affects existing sector output capacity. These are aggregate subaccounts, not independently audited corporations: credit, payroll, profits, bankruptcies and entry are still calculated at sector level. Sector capital and employment are not duplicated. Numerical responses and initial composition are assumptions and are not country data.

The pension fund and one aggregated investment fund hold deposits and acquire existing privately owned bank shares from households. These secondary purchases transfer deposits and ownership; they are not new equity issuance or physical investment. Private bank dividends are distributed to current owners; equity is revalued through the existing bank equity and resolution mechanism. Pension and investment fund accounts equal their deposits plus bank shares. The investment fund receives voluntary saving from available household liquidity; limited cash can be withdrawn when liquidity is tight. Pension shares can be sold when cash is needed and households can purchase them. Portfolios do not currently include corporate shares or bonds; there is no promise of returns or government guarantee. Household wealth includes its fund units, without counting the underlying bank shares again.

Voluntary private medical insurance collects affordable premiums and reimburses a portion of actually delivered private medical services. Claims are limited by fund cash. Health premiums and reimbursements are actual household/fund transfers; gross healthcare consumption and GDP remain recorded once. Participation and payment proportions are simplified scenario assumptions: no individual illness, actuarial underwriting, public health-insurance system or insurer competition is simulated. Turning off new insurance ends new premiums and claims.

Income distribution remains a synthetic within-stratum approximation. Revision 11 computes the richest tenth's income share from its weighted distribution, including within-group differences, rather than ranking only the ten averages. No individual survey is inferred. Existing saves migrate on the next month; old journal equations remain replayable. All amounts are subject to existing accounting checks.
