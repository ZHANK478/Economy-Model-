# Parliamentary party game, revision 1

New parliamentary-democracy games opt into `parliamentaryRevision: 1`. Old journals, presidential games and other government presets retain their existing rules. The opening scenario draws 2–10 distinct ideologies from all 13 available families, including the player's chosen family. Opening support is a saved, seeded scenario distribution, fitted to the common electorate calculator; all parties are initially represented. Subsequent elections use the normal electoral system and threshold without protection for the player.

`executive.playerParty` is a permanent player identity. `executive.inOffice` remains the existing whole-game continuation flag; `primeMinisterParty`, the actual `coalition`, and the revision's phase determine who governs. Cabinet loss does not stop economic time. Zero seats for the player's party after an actual parliamentary election ends this mode. Sovereignty loss remains an existing separate ending. There are no presidential elections in a parliamentary republic.

The player has three months to negotiate a cabinet. Each prospective partner asks for one visible, bounded policy concession drawn from its existing programme. A compromise is a smaller step, with a stricter compatibility requirement. Negotiated and current agreements are separate. Nothing changes in the cabinet or the state policy until a majority-backed cabinet is confirmed. Concessions enter the same bills, roll calls, next-month implementation and actual-budget funding as ordinary policy. Formation itself does not create money, parliamentary seats or policy outcomes.

Agreements monitor the actual legal policy, allow implementation time, warn before withdrawal, and permit three months to resolve a breach. A failed ordinary bill is not a cabinet defeat. A cabinet without a majority can survive if no alternative majority can agree. Rival parties form cabinets according to seat support and programme compatibility. Their concessions and legislative initiatives use the same law and economic engine; executive instruments and external-conflict negotiations are handled by the actual cabinet. The coalition-compatibility thresholds and delays are explicit game assumptions, not estimated institutional constants.

In opposition the player retains the same party, seats, election prospects and policy programme. Legislative proposals remain available; executive powers, military orders and direct dissolution are unavailable. Supporting or opposing a presented bill determines the player's faction vote, not a presidential signature or veto. Other factions may still pass the bill. Unanswered initiatives are voted on automatically according to party positions, so opponents do not require permission from the player to govern. The player can return to power by negotiating a genuine majority between elections. Decision attribution and economic responsibility follow the actual cabinet.

Only after election results are installed do unsuccessful non-player parties retire. Replacements are drawn from available inactive ideologies, receive fresh identities and zero seats, and join the following campaign. Historical party records and election results remain immutable. Replacement draws are finite if there are fewer available ideologies than retiring parties. No monthly removal of zero-seat newcomers takes place.

The interface reuses Politics: a cabinet reading, existing parliament diagram, one latest event and party cards. The HUD replaces the absent presidential poll with cabinet-support seats and the player's party poll. The new-game dialog uses three stages of choice cards and retains native form values for access and save consistency.

Validation: tests cover randomized rosters, legislative votes in opposition, restricted executive access, formation and return to government, warnings/withdrawal, zero-seat endings, newcomer seats, import/replay and visual templates. Three seeded ten-year runs across multiple elections reconcile accounts and replay to identical GDP. These are implementation checks, not empirical political predictions.

## Exact opposition faction instructions

`factionControlRevision: 1` in new-game provenance enables exact faction control. Existing parliamentary games activate it with a recorded, free `activateFactionControl` command when opened; old journal votes retain their original equations. While the player is outside the cabinet leadership, every MP of the player's party obeys the explicit bill instruction, or the saved default instruction for automatic ballots, including complete abstention. There are no random absences or defections in that faction in this mode. Other factions retain their existing behavior. The default can be changed without consuming one of the month's policy decisions; the actual instruction and roll-call outcome remain replayable. A player's own submitted bill implies support unless an explicit bill vote says otherwise.

Coalition cards now distinguish accepting the displayed condition from proposing a smaller concession. Counteroffers show their actual policy value and are omitted when slider rounding yields the same concession. Partner agreement remains a draft commitment until the player confirms a majority-backed cabinet; it does not itself change policy or grant executive power.
