# Defense economy: revision 10

The model is an aggregate fictional economy, not a forecast or a calibrated country model. Parameter values are scenario assumptions, not estimates taken from studies. Relevant evidence: [SIPRI definitions of military expenditure](https://www.sipri.org/databases/milex/definitions); [IMF World Economic Outlook 2026, chapter 2](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/april/english/ch2.pdf), especially fiscal tradeoffs and imported equipment; [NBER study of conscription in Portugal](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17694) and [Vietnam-era schooling and earnings](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13411). Their results are context-specific, not universal parameters.

The government chooses military funding, recruitment system, target personnel per thousand inhabitants, contract pay relative to the civilian wage, and procurement preference. Contract, mixed and conscription systems converge gradually to their assumed composition. Officers and contracted soldiers receive pay based on civilian wages, conscripts a smaller allowance. Actual gross payroll is capped by the military appropriation and available government funds. Income tax and pension contributions are collected from payroll; net pay reaches existing household groups. Soldiers are counted once as government employment and their remuneration once in government expenditure and value added. The model uses an assumed split of recruits from the civilian labor force and outside it; the latter can include students, but individual education histories are not simulated.

Military goods compete with other demand in the existing goods, currency and budget markets. Domestic orders go through existing sector enterprises and their ownership, production capacity, capital, wage and investment accounts; imported orders require foreign currency. Procurement preferences shift the aggregate government's import mix and are not a guarantee that every order is domestic or foreign. There is no separate weapons-factory agent or private combat contractor. Procurement and training use existing delivery delays; military forces, reserve and equipment evolve over time. War deaths, capital damage and an approximate stock of disabled veterans feed the economy; disability reduces available labor. Veteran-specific pensions, healthcare needs, individual schooling interruptions and detailed reserve cohorts are not separately modeled, and reserve composition is approximate.

Revision 9 and earlier remain replayable. At the next ordinary month they migrate to revision 10 without changing historical results; saves made immediately after activating defense are supported. Accounting diagnostics test that gross wages, taxes, contributions, household receipts and funded military goods are not counted twice.
